# Rehearsal: 10 minutes in the morning + the mock-judge drill

Slot: **8:30-9:15 (laptop clock), 3 timed judge runs** on the final deploy (team schedule). Do the 10-minute plan
once, then two more stopwatch runs of the judge visit. The partner does it again alone at ~1:30 PM at WEL, before the
first judge.

## The 10-minute plan

| Min | What | Pass when |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-1:00 | Each of you says the 30-second pitch out loud once, with a timer (`pitch/CHEAT_SHEET.md`). | Under 35 s, no reading. |
| 1:00-1:30 | Say the 3 lines from memory, each of you. | Word for word. |
| 1:30-7:30 | **One stopwatch run of a judge visit.** Partner = host (solo, as at 2 PM). CD + a friend or a spare phone = the judges. `/tv?judge=1&bots=1`, from hello to the close. Write down the clock at Start, at the reveal and at the close. | The reveal before 5:30 from hello; the cap holds at 3 turns; the partner speaks only names and coaching lines during turns. |
| 7:30-9:30 | **Drill:** the CD reads 5 questions from the list below at random; the partner answers each in under 20 s. | No "um, I think"; nothing from the never-say list. |
| 9:30-10:00 | Read the never-say list once. Agree on one thing to fix. | |

**If the run goes past 6:00 from hello:** tell the lead. The fallback is judge mode with max 2 turns (PITCH_KIT.md
section 10).

## The mock-judge drill: 15 hard questions

Answer in under 20 seconds. Bold = the line to land.

1. **"Isn't this just ChatGPT with dice?"**
   "A chatbot is one person and a screen. This is a room: one shared table, phones as controllers, and **the best move
   is using a friend's idea**. Muse is the host so the humans talk to each other."

2. **"What would break if you removed the AI?"**
   "Everything. Muse picks safe, playable stories, writes the opening, judges every plan, writes every turn and paints
   every scene. **Take Muse away and there's no game.** Code only does the numbers."

3. **"How do I know the AI isn't rigging the rolls?"**
   "It can't touch them. **The dice are crypto random numbers in code.** Muse only picks a difficulty label, and you
   can see every call in DM's Notes. Press N."

4. **"Was that story really from the news?"**
   "Yes. The start is a real article, shown at the end. **At this table it's a saved one from our checked library, so
   you never wait.** At home, Quick Tale writes a live opener from this week's odd news."

5. **"Real news can be dark. How is this safe?"**
   "Two gates. A keyword block, then Muse scores each story: no deaths, injuries, crime, politics or health scares.
   It has to average 4 of 5 on 7 storyability checks. **It's a problem to solve, never a villain or a victim.**"

6. **"What stops a player typing something awful?"**
   "Player text goes in as something your hero says, inside a strict format. **We threw 32 hostile scenarios at the
   DM.** If a check fails, the game uses a safe canned line. And the AI judges plans, never people."

7. **"Where does the human connection actually come from? Everyone's on their phone."**
   "The phone is only a controller; everyone looks at one TV. **On your turn, your friends pitch you ideas and you
   pick one.** You just argued about [their idea]. That's the point."

8. **"Doesn't the loudest person win?"**
   "No. One owner per turn, everyone gets a turn, typing is the default so the quiet person isn't shouted over, and
   **in the finale you can't vote for your own plan**."

9. **"Why is the narrator not a Meta model?"**
   "Meta has no text-to-speech model; Muse Voice Transcribe only listens. **Everything else is Meta:** Spark, Image,
   Transcribe and the Muse connector. Meta's own rep told us to pick the best model per job."

10. **"How did you test that the story makes sense?"**
    "A test harness plays whole games against real Muse calls with tricky plans and checks every outcome against the
    rules. And we watched our playtest videos frame by frame. **Our worst run is why we threw out the script.**"

11. **"What did AI do badly while you built it, and how did you fix it?"**
    "It wrote jargon. A player asked 'what the hell is foam?' Now every line is short and plain, cause then effect.
    And **a better rubric took Muse Spark from 5 of 8 fair rulings to 8 of 8**: the model was fine, our prompt wasn't."

12. **"Would people actually play this twice?"**
    "Every day brings new odd news, and Our stories turns your own lives into the story. **Next is Campaign mode:**
    your heroes carry over, like a real D&D group."

13. **"How much does a game cost to run?"**
    "**About 20 cents for a Quick Tale, by our math** from published prices. Paintings are about a cent each, and
    ready-made heroes are free. We haven't measured a real game yet." (Never say it as a measured fact.)

14. **"What happens to what we typed?"**
    "It goes to Meta's Muse to run tonight's game, and the phone says so. **No accounts, no player database.** The
    keepsake lives in the link. We never use Meta's contributor models; the server refuses to start with one."

15. **"What's the hardest thing you built?"**
    "Making a story follow players instead of a script. **Muse takes your plan literally and writes what would really
    follow**, and code checks it kept your words and never undid a win. One retry, then a fallback, so the table never
    freezes."

**If you don't know:** "Good question. I don't want to guess. [Partner/CD] built that part; can I follow up after?"
Never invent a number.
