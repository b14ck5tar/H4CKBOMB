# VIDEO SCRIPT: Group Project (Meta track video, final build)

*Group Project: the one group project where everyone actually shows up.*

Rewritten Sun Oct 25 ~8:45 AM for the **procedural story build**: players steer and the DM reacts, "tell the table
your plan", friends' ideas (+1), swipe to throw, graded rolls, a new painting per decision, the hand-off to the next
player by name, 3 acts, the shake-together finale and "Based on a true story".
**Record on the deployed build (groupproject-game.vercel.app) once it runs the final branch. Devpost closes 11:30 AM;
the CD leaves at 11 AM.**
Never on screen or in the words: secret clues, Convince the DM, Boost, Catch!, Mirror, hearts / Hope, "Needs 12+",
"fairytale", "inspired by real events", "this really happened", "weirdly good at" (the question is now "What's your
secret talent?").

## Shot key

- **LIVE**: the CD (or the partner) must record it with real people in the room. Nobody else can.
- **SCREEN**: a screen recording of the TV or a phone during the live take (it runs at the same time as LIVE).
- **PREP**: can be made ahead without people: stills from `devpost-media/gallery-v2/`, the deck, a screen capture.

not shots. Burn in captions for every spoken line (judges often watch muted).

**Who reads:** the creative director, in their own voice, like telling a friend. No trailer voice, no
"revolutionary", "seamless", "game-changing", "powered by AI". No em dashes in captions.

| How long? | **Quick** (~5 min) | Opens on a hand-picked true story from our library: no vote, a known-good story |

Test players have names like Riley and Sam. Never call one "my friend" or imply it's a person.

**Do 2 full takes.** Keep the one where (a) the turn owner picks the other human's idea, (b) one roll lands low (UH OH
or SORT OF) and one lands high (GREAT or LEGENDARY), (c) the outcome clearly uses the player's own words, and (d) the
reveal headline is easy to read.

**Plan the moves, not the words:**
- Human A's first turn: Human B types a suggestion and argues for it out loud. A picks it (+1, B's name on screen).
- Human B's first turn: B reads their plan to the table first (the TV prompts it), then sends it.
- Plans with one clear, concrete noun work best (a ladder, a cinnamon roll, a marching band). The story keeps that
  noun.

## Spoken script only (for the read)

**Part 1 (63 words, ~28 s)**
When friends hang out, a lot of the night is everyone on their own phone.
Tabletop games fix that. Everyone gets a role and a reason to talk. But someone has to run the game, so most groups
never play.
So we built Group Project. Meta's Muse is the Dungeon Master, your phones are the controllers, and every story is
based on a true story.

**Part 2 (159 words, ~73 s; the rest is game sound)**
Scan the code. No app, no account. Describe your hero in one line, and tell it your secret talent.
Muse turns a real, odd news story into tonight's adventure, casts a narrator and paints us in.
There's no script. The players steer. On your turn, you tell the table your plan, and your friends pitch you ideas.
Use one and you get plus one, with their name on screen.
Swipe up to throw. Every number is its own outcome, from disaster to legendary. Code rolls the dice, never the AI.
Muse takes your plan literally and writes what would really happen next. Every decision gets a new painting, and the
story hands the spotlight to the next friend by name.
For the finale, everyone backs one plan, then shakes their phone at the same time. One die for the whole table.
Then the twist. It's based on a true story: the real article, what happened next, and how your secret talent saved
the day.

**Part 3 (64 words, ~29 s)**
Muse runs the night. Muse Spark picks news that makes a good story, writes the opening, judges every plan and writes
every turn. Muse Image paints your heroes into each new scene.
Code keeps it honest. It rolls the dice, keeps every win, and checks the story uses your words. The AI judges ideas,
never people.
The one non-Meta piece is the narrator's voice, because Meta has no text-to-speech model.

**Part 4 (66 words, ~30 s)**
This is my first hackathon. I made the creative calls, and Claude Code was our lead engineer, running a team of
agents.
They watched our playtest videos frame by frame. Our worst run showed the story ignoring players, so we rebuilt it
around their choices.
A test harness plays whole games to check every turn, and a bake-off showed a better rubric beat a bigger model.

If Part 4 runs long, drop "running a team of agents" (the screen shows them).

**Facts behind each line (so nobody overclaims):**
- "Players steer, the DM reacts", literal plans, the player's own words, progress, 3 acts, the hand-off by name:
  design/35 and `game/src/lib/procedural.ts` (`checkTurn`, `applyTurn`).
- "Tell the table your plan": `components/QuickTV.tsx` (shown while the turn owner types).
- Grades: `lib/grade.ts` (DISASTER 1, UH OH 2-5, SORT OF 6-9, IT WORKS 10-14, GREAT 15-19, LEGENDARY 20+).
- "+1 with their name": `lib/host.ts` (`+1 friend's idea`); finale backers add +1 each, max +2.
- "Picks news that makes a good story": the storyability gate, `server/news/candidates.ts` (7 scores, average 4+,
  none below 3).
- "Based on a true story" and "Solved by" (built in code from the join answers): `lib/host.ts`, `tvskin/EndingMontage.tsx`.
- "Frame by frame": design/33 (a frame every 0.5 s, 1,131 frames, transcribed with Muse Transcribe).
- "A test harness": `scripts/procedural-test.mjs`; last logged run in design/35 (every outcome used the player's
  idea, 0 fallbacks, every game reached act 3).
- "A better rubric beat a bigger model": design/23 (Spark minimal, rubric v1 5-7/8 → v2 8/8 in 3 runs).

## Recording checklist

### Before the camera rolls (Sun ~9:30 to 10:15 AM)
- [ ] The live site runs the final build (see DEVPOST.md, check 1). Record on that URL.
- [ ] Check on the deployed build; if one is missing, change the line, not the footage:
  - "{name}, tell the table your plan" while the owner types. *Missing:* say "On your turn, your friends pitch you ideas."
  - The next player named at the end of an outcome. *Missing:* drop "and the story hands the spotlight to the next friend by name."
  - "Based on a true story" on the reveal. *Missing:* say "Then the twist. The story was real."
  - Narrator audio plays (not text only). Listen once.
- [ ] One warm-up game first (wakes Muse and the narrator).
- [ ] DM's Notes (N) lists only current rules.
- [ ] Laptop: Do Not Disturb, close Slack/Discord/email (a Discord DM popped up in a past recording), hide the
      bookmarks bar, browser full screen (F11), zoom 100%.
- [ ] Phones: Do Not Disturb, brightness up, battery over 50%, ringer off but game sound on.
- [ ] No keys, `.env.local`, Vercel env pages or tokens anywhere on screen, including the Claude Code capture.
- [ ] Everyone on camera says OK out loud on the recording.

### TV capture (laptop)
- **OBS (preferred):** 1920x1080, 60 fps, MP4 (or MKV then File > Remux), NVENC/QuickSync or x264 "veryfast", CBR
  15,000 Kbps. Source: Window Capture of the browser or Display Capture. Desktop Audio ON, Mic OFF.
- **Fallback: Snipping Tool.** Win+Shift+R, drag the full screen, system audio ON, mic OFF.

### Phone captures
- **iPhone:** Control Center > Screen Recording, long-press to turn the mic off. Start 3 s early.
- **Android:** Quick Settings > Screen record, device audio, "Show touches" ON (shows the swipe).
- Portrait, one recording per phone for the whole game.

### Room camera (LIVE shots)
- A third phone on a tripod or a stack of books, landscape, 1080p 30 fps, wide enough for both faces and the TV glow,
  plus one close-up of the two players leaning over one phone.
- **Clap once on camera at the start** with every recording running. The clap syncs the tracks in the edit.

### Voiceover
- A small quiet room (a closet with clothes is ideal). Phone voice memo, mic about 6 inches away, a little off to the side.
- Read each part twice, then one full read. 2 s pause between takes. Read slower than feels natural.

### Edit and export
- Game sound under the VO at about -18 dB; up to about -10 dB on the narrator and the reveal.
- The game's music is original (generated in code), so no extra music is needed.
- Burn in captions; keep text out of the bottom 15% of the frame.
- Export 1080p H.264 MP4, at or under 3:00. Watch it once on a phone, once on a laptop, once muted.
- Upload (YouTube unlisted). Open the link in a private window to confirm it plays without a login. Paste it into
  Devpost before 11:30 AM. Keep the MP4 on the laptop and a phone as the backup for judging.
