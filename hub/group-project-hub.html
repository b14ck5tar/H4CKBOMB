The 30-second pitch (say it while they scan)

"Last time you hung out with friends, how much of it was everyone on their own phone? Game nights fix that, but someone has to run the game, so nobody plays. Here, Meta's Muse is the Dungeon Master. The TV is the table, your phone is the controller. On your turn your friends send you ideas, and using one is your best move. You swipe to throw the die, Muse paints what happens, and at the end you find out it was based on a true story. Scan this."

The visit, in order

  1. QR card first, then the pitch. "It's a whole game in about five minutes. The best part is the last 30 seconds."
  2. Heroes: "Muse is painting you as a cartoon. Your secret talent comes back at the end."
  3. Start. Over the opener, once: "Muse wrote this and painted you in." Then quiet.
  4. Turn 1: "Everyone else, send them an idea. Use a friend's and you get plus one." Then: "Which one made you laugh?"
  5. "Swipe up to throw." On the meter: "Every number is its own outcome. Code rolls, never the AI."
  6. Finale: "Everyone back one plan, not your own. Then everyone shakes at once."
  7. Reveal: wait 3 seconds. "That's the real headline." "That's the talent you typed." "Take it home."
  8. Close: "Your friends' ideas were your best move. Muse wrote, judged and painted every decision; code rolled the dice. It was based on a true story. And you just played it."
  9. For Meta judges, last: "Every brain in this game is Meta's Muse: it writes, judges every plan, paints you in, and listens. The only thing it borrowed is a voice."

Top 5 judge questions

What does the AI actually do?

"Muse Spark picks odd news that's safe and playable, writes the opening, judges every plan and writes every turn. Muse Image paints your heroes into each new scene. Press N: every call and why. Code rolls the dice. Take Muse away and there's no game."

Is the story real?

"The starting event is a real article, or a player's own true story. Today's table plays a saved one from our checked library, so you never wait. Everything after the start is your plans plus Muse."

What about dark news?

"A keyword block, then Muse scores every story: no deaths, injuries, crime, politics or health scares. It has to average 4 of 5 on 7 storyability checks. Heavy news is a future opt-in mode."

How did you use AI to build it?

"Claude Code ran a team of agents: builders, testers, reviewers. They watched our playtest videos frame by frame; our worst run is why we rebuilt the story around players' choices. A better rubric took Muse Spark from 5 of 8 fair rulings to 8 of 8."

What's next?

"Campaign mode: your heroes carry over into the next story, like real D&D. Then Our stories as the main mode, and an opt-in Real World mode with guardrails."

The DM reactsMuse writes what really follows, a new painting fades in, the next player is named.
  • 1 DISASTER
  • 2-5 UH OH
  • 6-9 SORT OF
  • 10-14 IT WORKS
  • 15-19 GREAT

Then the finale: everyone proposes, everyone backs a plan that isn't theirs, and the whole table shakes their phones for one shared die. The ending credits every hero by name, then: Based on a true story, the real article, and "Solved by" each player's secret talent.
