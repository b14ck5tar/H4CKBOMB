# 13: The Judge Demo (4 minutes, leave anytime)

Sat 2026-10-24. Goal: stranger judges laugh hard, learn one true thing about each other, pull it off together, never feel trapped, and want to come back. **[V]** = DOI or page checked. **Hedge** = weaker than its pop reputation, so don't claim it on stage.

## 1. Findings, each with one implication

1. **Judges get about 2 minutes of demo.** MLH's science-fair plan budgets ~4 min per project: 2 for the demo, 1 for questions and scoring, 1 for travel [V] https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/judging-and-submissions/judging-plan. Show the thing, skip backstory (Eden 2019 [V] https://shkspr.mobi/blog/2019/11/how-to-present-at-a-hack-day/). **→ Play lasts 3:15 at most and leaves time for questions. Thesis goes under the joining step.**
2. **Peak-end rule.** Memory of an episode tracks its peak and end, not its length (Fredrickson & Kahneman 1993 [V] https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.65.1.45; Kahneman et al. 1993 [V] https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1993.tb00589.x). **Hedge:** it didn't replicate for simple pleasant experiences (Mah & Bernstein 2019 [V] https://doi.org/10.1037/h0101838). **→ Still a cheap bet: end on the warmest moment (Mirror) and the keepsake landing on their phone.**
3. **"Last" raises enjoyment.** An item labeled last was enjoyed more (O'Brien & Ellsworth 2012 [V] https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611427408). **→ Say "Last roll."**
4. **Curiosity gaps.** Curiosity peaks when you know enough to see what's missing (Loewenstein 1994 [V] https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75). **→ Show the strange headline, hold the real story until the reveal, and have the keepsake tease Chapter 2.**
5. **Open loops, stated honestly.** A 2025 meta-analysis found **no** memory advantage for unfinished tasks (the Zeigarnik effect), but a robust tendency to **resume** them (the Ovsiankina effect) (Ghibellini & Meier 2025 [V] https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-025-05000-w). **→ Don't count on them remembering us. Make resuming one tap: a link that's already on their phone.**
6. **Reactance and autonomy.** People push back when their freedom feels threatened (Steindl et al. 2015 [V] https://doi.org/10.1027/2151-2604/a000222). Autonomy fuels intrinsic motivation (Ryan & Deci 2000 [V] https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68). "But you are free" about doubled compliance (Carpenter 2013 [V] https://doi.org/10.1080/10510974.2012.727941). **Hedge:** low-risk-of-bias studies showed no effect (Fillon et al. 2023 [V] https://doi.org/10.15626/MP.2020.2640). **→ Say "leave anytime" because it's true: the hero plays on and nothing waits for them.**
7. **Start with a tiny ask (foot in the door).** People who say yes to a small request are more likely to say yes to a bigger one (Freedman & Fraser 1966 [V] https://doi.org/10.1037/h0023552). **→ The first ask is tapping a face (2 s). Typing comes later.**
8. **Small choice sets.** Offering 6 options led to more choosing than offering 24 (Iyengar & Lepper 2000 [V] https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.6.995). The overload effect grows under time pressure (Chernev et al. 2015 [V] https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2014.08.002). **→ Offer exactly 6 ready-made heroes plus a "make my own" tile.**
9. **IKEA and endowment effects.** People value what they helped build (Norton et al. 2012 [V] https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2011.08.002) and what they own (Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler 1990 [V] https://doi.org/10.1086/261737). **→ A ready-made hero plus one line of their own ("secretly afraid of ___") makes the hero theirs. The keepsake is theirs to keep.**
10. **Disclosure and compliments.** Talking about yourself is intrinsically rewarding (Tamir & Mitchell 2012 [V] https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1202129109). People underestimate how good a compliment feels to the person who gets it (Boothby & Bohns 2021 [V] https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167220949003). **→ The quirk line is a tiny, funny disclosure. Mirror turns it into "a stranger noticed me."**
11. **Laughter is social.** Laughs occur mostly during social encounters (Provine & Fischer 1989 [V] https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1439-0310.1989.tb00536.x). Social laughter tracks bonding (Dunbar et al. 2012 [V] https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2011.1373). **→ The laugh is the shared TV stamping a judge's own words. The CD reads the best one aloud.**
12. **The honeypot effect.** People using a display draw in passers-by (Brignull & Rogers 2003, INTERACT; Wouters et al. 2016 [V] https://doi.org/10.1145/2901790.2901796). Seeing others play raised the share of passers-by who joined by 136% (Müller et al. 2014 [V] https://doi.org/10.1145/2556288.2557001). Arcades' attract mode does this on purpose (https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/AttractMode). **→ The TV is never idle: it loops highlights, or the team plays live.**
13. **Museum holding power.** Across 110 exhibitions, visits averaged ~20 min. Smaller exhibitions were used more thoroughly, and crowded ones got stops at fewer than a quarter of their elements (Serrell [V] https://informalscience.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/SA.PA2.FinalDforCAISE2016.pdf). Visitors weigh benefit against cost in seconds, and cutting cost is easier than raising benefit (Bitgood 2014 [V] https://www.aam-us.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/4EXHSP14Bitgood_ExhibitionDesignthatprovidesHighValues.pdf). **→ Show 5 mechanics, not 10. State the cost first: "3-minute tale. Tap a hero."**
14. **Party-game onboarding.** Jackbox limits input to draw, tap or type, gives one task at a time, designs for the least experienced player, and acts as host (Built In [V] https://builtin.com/media-gaming/jackbox-games-design-party-pack). Drop-in/drop-out co-op uses bots so the game survives when someone leaves (https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Concepts/Drop_In_Drop_Out). **→ One big button per phone screen, no rules talk, and autopilot heroes.**

## 2. The flow (Quick Tale: 3:15 of play, then Q&A)

Between judges, the TV runs the attract loop or the team plays live. The tent card reads **"3-minute tale · on your phone · leave anytime."** A team "house hero" is always seated, so even a single judge has a table.

| Time | Phones | CD says | TV |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Scan the QR | "Want a 3-minute tale? Scan. Leave whenever. Your hero keeps playing." | Lobby, 6 seats |
| 0:10 | Tap 1 of 6 heroes, then pick or type a quirk: "secretly afraid of ___" | Over the tapping: "Rooms stay quiet because we're unsure, not scared. Games give everyone a role, a reason and a script. Someone has to prep as DM, so **Muse is the DM.**" | Heroes pop in instantly with their quirks |
| 0:35 | (wait) | "Real news from this week, filtered for anything sad. An 80-ton boulder fell in a New Hampshire park. Nobody was hurt. Tonight, we find out why." | Quest card. The real answer is held back |
| 0:50 | Secret clue | "Nobody else has your clue. Say it out loud." The house hero goes first, then the CD **stops talking.** | "Clues are secret. Talk." |
| 1:05 | Type a plan (25 s, help chips after 8 s) | (silent) | Verdict stamps land one at a time |
| 1:30 | Harshest ruling: **Convince the DM** (15 s) | "One shot. Argue with your DM." | **LAUGH 1:** the CD reads the Absurd plan aloud. **LAUGH 2:** the appeal flips the stamp |
| 1:50 | Roll | "**Last roll.** Everyone at once." On a fail: "Spend your heart to re-roll a stranger's fail." | Dice, then a group banner: "The boulder goes home" (**pulled it off together**) |
| 2:10 | Ready | Reads the best beat aloud | One beat per hero, quoting their words (**LAUGH 3**) |
| 2:25 | Ready | 5 s of silence | **Reveal:** "The official story" vs "What your group uncovered" |
| 2:40 | Mirror: "You secretly guarded [hero]. One kind line about something they did." Chips or type, 20 s | "Nobody rates anyone." | Quill animation |
| 3:00 | (read) | Lets it land | **Mirror reveal**, all cards at once: **"I learned something about them"** |
| 3:15 | Keepsake page: the tale, their hero, and "Chapter 2 at 4:30, table N" | "That's yours, on your phone. Come back at 4:30 and see what the next table does with your hero." | Storybook cover and QR |
| 3:25 | (free) | Four-line scorecard in one breath (see PITCH_CARD), then questions | Highlights loop; DM's Notes ready |

**Leave-anytime design:**
- **Phone footer:** "Leave anytime · your hero plays on · your tale: [link]."
- **Autopilot:** a phone that's idle 10 s, or disconnected, plays on with a canned in-character plan and an auto-roll. Gates stop waiting for that player.
- **Keepsake link:** live from the moment they join, and shows the finished tale later.
- **Everyone leaves:** the house hero finishes; the tale feeds the attract loop.
- **Rushed judges:** ENG hotkeys from the roll to the reveal.

## 3. Product changes, ranked (S < 1 h, M 1–3 h, L > 3 h)

1. **Quick Tale mode** (`/tv?mode=quick`), **M.** Runs, in order:
   - one scene (the climax), with a 25 s plan window
   - an appeal for the lowest ruling only (15 s)
   - roll and Catch, then the beats and the reveal
   - Mirror with a 20 s write, **no guess phase**, and every card revealed at once
   - the keepsake (no epilogue)

   A hard cap jumps to the reveal at 3:00.
2. **Six ready-made heroes**, **M.** Pre-paint the portraits now (6 × ~14 s, pennies) and add quirk chips. The "make my own" tile paints its portrait in the background. Drafts for the CD: night-shift librarian, ghost-tour guide who's never seen a ghost, pigeon whisperer, retired cryptid hunter, overqualified intern, grandma with a metal detector.
3. **Presence and autopilot**, **M.** Track who is active, and make the Ready, all-pitched and all-rolled checks count only active heroes. Today a judge who walks away stalls the table until the safety timer fires. It also enables "add house hero."
4. **Keepsake link** (`/tale/<id>`), **M.** Save the storybook as a Supabase row, and show "Keep my tale" from the moment they join. Today the shelf exists only in the TV's localStorage.
5. **Attract screen**, **S.** After 20 s idle, loop recent stamps and reveals with a big QR and "3-minute tale · leave anytime." Show only hero names and DM text, never Mirror lines.
6. **Demo hotkeys**, **S.** Add skip-to-reveal, force-fail (for Catch) and add-house-hero. PITCH_CARD assumes hotkeys exist, but TV.tsx handles only Space, N and Esc.
7. **Judges' Saga** (opt-in), **M.** Earlier groups' heroes make cameo appearances in the next quest's DM context. At 4:30 the TV plays a 60 s finale, one quoted line per saved tale. This is the payoff for coming back.
8. **Help chips for the Mirror write**, **S.** A blank kind-sentence box for a stranger is the scariest input in the game.

## 4. What the CD says, and what to avoid

**Say:**
- "3-minute tale. Scan. Leave whenever."
- "Say your clue out loud." (then stop talking)
- "One shot. Argue with your DM."
- "Last roll. Everyone at once."
- "Nobody rates anyone."
- "That's yours, on your phone. Come back at 4:30 if you like."

**Avoid:**
- "Before we start, let me explain the rules..." The first scene teaches the game.
- "One more thing" or "Wait, don't go." Both trigger reactance.
- Apologizing for latency or missing features.
- Typing on a judge's phone, or talking over the clue exchange or the reveal.
- Hedged claims stated as fact: "studies prove," Zeigarnik ("you can't stop thinking about it"), "one night cures loneliness."
- Asking for compliments out loud. Mirror is written and private until the reveal.
- Ending on the scorecard. Say it, then point them back to their phones.
