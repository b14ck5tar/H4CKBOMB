## 1. In-person vs online socializing

- **Socializing time is collapsing, fastest among the young.** In the American Time Use Survey, 15–24-year-olds went from 61 to 35 minutes a day of in-person socializing between 2003 and 2025 (−43%). All ages fell from 47 to 35 minutes. [V] Hedgeye summary of ATUS: https://app.hedgeye.com/insights/184658-howe-america-s-social-life-is-shrinking ; Axios: https://www.axios.com/2026/07/05/americans-socializing-decline
- **Staying in is now the default, mainly because of cost.** 68% of Gen Z say going out isn't worth what it costs. 73% say staying in is their default plan, and 74% stay in at least half their weekends. They avoid big groups of strangers (39%), loud venues (38%) and alcohol-centred nights (35%). (Harris Poll, July 2026.) [P] https://theharrispoll.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/The-Gen-Z-Weekend-Report-July-2026.pdf
- **Game nights are the top choice for an interactive night out.** 56% of 18–34-year-olds gravitate toward game and trivia nights, and those event types grew 45% (Eventbrite and Harris). [V] https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/?p=433955
- **Board game events on Partiful quadrupled year over year** (NYT, via YPulse, Dec 2024). [V, secondhand] https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2024/12/18/gen-z-and-millennials-are-joining-board-game-clubs/
- **Run clubs show the appetite for structured, low-stakes group rituals.** Running clubs grew 59% on Strava in 2024, and 1 in 5 Gen Z respondents had gone on a date with someone they met at one. This is company data. [P] https://athletechnews.com/strava-2024-fitness-report-highlights-trends
- **The TTRPG boom is real, but there is no audited player count.** Wizards of the Coast claims more than 85M fans and more than 18M D&D Beyond users, and the 2024 Player's Handbook was the fastest-selling D&D product ever. [U, secondary] https://voxbooster.com/blog/dnd-ttrpg-statistics-2026. Baldur's Gate 3 peaked at 814,666 concurrent players on Steam. [V] https://hitmarker.net/news/baldurs-gate-3s-concurrent-player-count-peaks-above-800-000-players-on-steam-2232838
- **The DM bottleneck.** Many players can't find a game master. In one informal poll, the most common answer was "I wish I had a DM," and the "forever DM" is a well-known pain point. [U, informal] https://www.greyhawkgrognard.com/?p=26865 ; https://www.enworld.org/threads/the-dm-shortage.693711/
- **Games are a primary social channel.** 74% of players play with others, and 79% of Gen Alpha and Gen Z play with friends (ESA 2025). [V, secondary] https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/esa-the-american-games-industry-in-2025 . 40% of Gen Z and millennials socialize more in games than in the physical world (Deloitte DMT 2025). [V] https://gamesbeat.com/young-generations-consume-a-tapestry-of-games-and-other-media-deloitte/

**Read:** people want to be together, but cheaply, at home, in small known groups, with a structure that gives the night a shape. "Game night at home with a host built in" sits right in that gap.

**Pattern.** People **return** for small known groups, a ritual cadence (daily, weekly or yearly), sessions under about 5 minutes, a spoiler-free share artifact, and fresh content each time. People **leave** because of performative pressure, individual push guilt, needing everyone live for a long time, novelty with no new content, and friction such as a second app or invites.

## 3. Attitudes toward AI in social and creative contexts

- **Concern outweighs excitement.** 50% of Americans are more concerned than excited about AI, 10% more excited. Concern has risen from 37% in 2021 (Pew, Jun 2025, n=5,023). [P] https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/how-americans-view-ai-and-its-impact-on-people-and-society/
- **Relationships and creativity are where people expect harm.** 53% expect AI to worsen creative thinking, and 50% expect it to worsen people's ability to form meaningful relationships. [P] same Pew report.
- **Keep AI out of judging people.** 66% say AI should not judge whether two people could fall in love, and 73% say it should have no role in faith advice. [P] same Pew report; https://www.axios.com/2025/09/17/pew-research-american-ai-survey-2025
- **People want disclosure.** 76% say it is very or extremely important to tell AI content from human content, and 53% doubt they can. [V] https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2025/53727/americans-more-concerned-than-excited-about-ai-study-pew
- **Comfort rises with human control (Reuters Institute 2025, 6 countries):** 62% are comfortable with all-human news, 43% with human-led news that uses AI help, 21% with AI-led news under human oversight, and about 12% with fully AI news. Soft topics are more acceptable than politics. [V] https://reporterzy.info/en/5307,journalism-in-the-age-of-ai-why-people-prefer-humans-over-machines
- **Gamers are hostile to generative AI, especially for art and creative work.** 85% are below neutral and 63% chose "most negative" (Quantic Foundry, n=1,799, self-selected). Attitudes are less negative when AI is used *within gameplay*, and most negative among narrative-loving, younger and female players. [V] https://www.gamesmarket.global/quantic-foundry-most-gamers-are-negative-about-the-use-of-gen-ai-in-video-games ; https://www.statista.com/statistics/1664229/attitudes-towards-genai-in-video-games-by-use-case/
- **"Slop" was Merriam-Webster's 2025 Word of the Year**, defined as low-quality AI content produced in quantity. The tone is mockery, and the president said it reflects a desire for authentic content. [V] https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/merriam-websters-word-of-the-year-for-2025-is-slop-the-ai-generated-junk-that-fills-our-social-media-feeds-180987887/
- **The AI-companion backlash is public.** The Friend pendant's $1M NYC subway campaign was defaced with "AI is not your friend" and "Talk to a neighbor." [V] https://krdo.com/news/2025/11/16/how-this-tiny-device-became-a-symbol-for-the-backlash-against-ai/
- **Teens use AI companions but prefer people.** 72% have tried one, yet 67% find them less satisfying than human conversation (Common Sense Media, n=1,060). [V] https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/21/72-of-u-s-teens-have-used-ai-companions-study-finds/
- **TTRPG institutions are anti-AI.** The ENnies bar generative-AI products from 2025–26, and WotC bans AI in final products. [V] https://aicentral.substack.com/p/the-tabletop-paradox

**Read:** AI is welcome as a **host or tool that brings humans together** (like a DM who frees a friend to play). It is rejected when it **replaces people**, **judges people**, or **floods the experience with generic content**. Disclosure and human control raise acceptance in measurable steps.

## 4. What people dislike and love in party and group games

**Dislikes** (design literature and journalism; no single large survey exists, so this section leans on qualitative sources):
- **Downtime and player elimination** are the classic social-game failures. Fixes include known time limits and keeping eliminated players involved as spectators. [V] https://math.vu.nl/~eliens/demo/pattern-downtime.html ; https://math.vu.nl/~eliens/demo/pattern-playerelimination.html
- **Quarterbacking (alpha players)** in co-op games: one player directs everyone and others lose agency. Fixes include private information on each player's own device and per-player choices. [V] https://www.meeplemountain.com/articles/benching-the-quarterback-how-to-deal-with-alpha-players-in-co-op-games/
- **Setup friction:** a second app, Wi-Fi setup and controllers. "If setup takes too long, folks lose interest." [V] https://www.pocketgamer.com/the-jackbox-party-pack-12/interview-with-allard-laban-cco/ ; https://kotaku.com/the-jackbox-party-games-are-some-of-the-best-games-of-t-1840661742
- **Being put on the spot and social anxiety:** Gen Z avoids large groups of strangers and loud, high-pressure settings (Harris, above). BeReal users described sudden "pressure" when prompted. [V]
- **Mean-spirited dynamics:** Gas grew *because* it was compliment-only, which suggests kindness is a draw for teens. [V, inference]
- **Long rules explanations, walls of reading, account signup:** [U] These are widely cited by designers and reviewers, but I found no hard data in this pass.

**Loves:** laughing together around a shared screen. Jackbox's first test drew crowds of diners and waitstaff. [P] https://www.pocketgamer.com/the-jackbox-party-pack-12/interview-with-allard-laban-cco/ . Other draws are a low barrier, everyone contributing at once, surprise (dice, reveals), a shareable artifact (Wordle, Wrapped) and short sessions (Wordle's "three minutes a day").

## 5. News attitudes (for the real-headline quests)

- **40% of people across 48 countries sometimes or often avoid the news**, up from 29% in 2017 (Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2025). [V] https://www.statista.com/chart/34635/why-people-actively-try-to-avoid-the-news
- **Why they avoid it:** 39% say it hurts their mood, 31% are worn out by the volume, and 29% each cite too much war or too much politics. Younger people more often say they feel powerless, that news is irrelevant or hard to follow, or that it "leads to arguments." [V] same source.
- **About 42% of 18–24s avoid the news.** They do care, but want it "more relevant, easier to understand and less gloomy." [V] https://www.politics.ox.ac.uk/news/young-audiences-are-redefining-how-they-consume-news-new-reuters-report-argues
- **News avoiders are more likely to want positive or solutions-based journalism** (DNR 2023). [V] https://www.solutionsjournalism.org/organizations/research ; https://www.inma.org/blogs/world-congress/post.cfm/journalism-focused-on-solutions-progress-can-help-re-engage-young-news-avoiders
- **AI handling soft news is more acceptable than AI handling politics** (Reuters 2025 GenAI report, above). [V]

**Read:** a quirky, constructive, non-political headline, told as a fairytale and then revealed as real, is a good fit for news avoiders. The two-stage tragedy filter is a selling point, not just a safety measure. Avoid war, politics and anything that "leads to arguments" at a family table.

## DESIGN IMPLICATIONS FOR ONCE UPON A ROLL

Effort is for a build with about 16 hours left: S is under 1 hour, M is 1–3 hours, L is more than 3 hours.

1. **Join with a QR code, a name and nothing else. No app, no account, no email.** Jackbox dropped its own app because of friction, and Clubhouse's invite gate hurt growth. Accounts belong only to the *party* (Bookshelf), created by the host with a party name. **S** (verify the M1 flow has no login step.)
2. **Make it visible that AI narrates and friends decide.** Show a persistent TV footer: "Narrated by AI · Decided by friends · Dice are real." Pew: 66% reject AI judging people. Reuters: comfort rises from 21% to 43% when humans lead. The verdict stamp only rates *feasibility* and never ranks people; the party vote and the hero in peril pick winners. **S**
3. **Disclose in the storybook.** The colophon reads "Written by The Cousins · Illustrated and narrated with AI." Pew: 76% want to tell AI from human work. Disclosure is cheap trust. **S**
4. **Quote players verbatim to fight "slop."** The storybook shows each winning pitch *in the player's own words* (bold, attributed), and the AI writes only the connecting tissue. Cap each narration beat at 2–3 sentences on the TV. Slop was Word of the Year, and gamers are most hostile to AI used for creative work and narrative. **M**
5. **One consistent illustrated style with no photoreal images.** Already in the spec (Illuminated Storybook, one style string). Keep it, and never generate realistic faces of real people from headlines. Quantic Foundry: art and creative uses are the most rejected. **S**
6. **Spoiler-free share card, like Wordle's grid.** Example: `Once Upon a Roll · The Cousins, Book 3 · 🎲 20 · 7 · 1💀 · 15 · Final 18 ✨ · Hope ❤️❤️🖤`, plus the titles earned. Make one-tap share an image, with a QR on the card and no spoiler of the real headline. Wordle grew from 90 to 300K players after its grid launched, and Wrapped drew 500M shares in a day. **M**
7. **Keep phones busy so no one waits.** During other players' rolls, phones offer react buttons and a one-tap "Predict: hit or miss?" whose results the narrator calls out. Downtime is the classic social-game killer. **S–M**
8. **Spotlight fairness against quarterbacking.** Break vote ties toward the player whose pitches have won least. Show a "Hasn't had the spotlight yet" glow on the Pitch Wall. Pitches stay private on each phone until the Wall reveals them all at once, so no one can steer them. Quarterbacking guides recommend private input and per-player agency. **S**
9. **Give a graceful "help me" button on the phone.** If a player has no idea by about 8 seconds, they get 3 one-tap suggestion chips in their character's voice, and voice input stays optional. Gen Z avoids put-on-the-spot, high-pressure settings, and BeReal-style "pressure" drives people away. **S**
10. **Make Catch! and the Rescue Round the emotional center, and keep roasts affectionate.** Prompt the roast line toward "fond teasing." Shame Titles are funny, never cruel. Add a "Family mode" toggle that softens roasts. Gas showed kindness spreads, and families need to keep the TV safe. **S**
11. **Frame the newsprint reveal as constructive.** End with "This really happened, and here's how it turned out," highlighting the helpful or delightful part of the story. Never use politics or war. 39% avoid news because of their mood, and young people want news that is "less gloomy." Avoiders lean toward solutions journalism. **S** (prompt change in the reveal step)
12. **Promise a short session.** The lobby says "Quick Tale · 4 min" or "Full Tale · ~15 min" before starting, with a hard end and no endless mode. Wordle takes about 3 minutes, and Clubhouse's long live rooms didn't survive normal life. **S**
13. **Make the weekly chapter a ritual, not a streak.** "Chapter 2 unlocks next week" with *no* individual push alerts and *no* streak loss. Missing a week is fine, because the shelf waits. Locket's weekly Rollcall gets more than 25% weekly participation, while BeReal's pressure alert was a pain point. **S** (copy only, for v1)
14. **Let latecomers and spectators join as the "Village Crowd."** Someone who joins mid-tale, or a shy cousin, can vote and react but doesn't pitch, and can be promoted to a hero next chapter. The fix for elimination and downtime is to keep people involved, and Jackbox-style audiences are proven. [U for the audience specifics] **M**
15. **Position it as "game night for $0, with the DM built in."** Use this in the pitch and on the landing page. 68% of Gen Z say going out isn't worth the cost, 56% of 18–34-year-olds want game nights, and "I wish I had a DM" is the hobby's common lament. **S**

## Red flags to avoid (things that make people leave)

- **Any signup, download or permission prompt before the fun starts**, including mic permission on first load. Ask for it only when the player taps push-to-talk.
- **Individual push notifications, streaks or guilt mechanics** (BeReal pressure, burnout).
- **AI that judges people, picks winners, or presents itself as a friend or companion.** Never say "your AI friend" (the Friend backlash, and Pew's 66%).
- **Walls of AI text, generic AI imagery, or a narrator that ignores what players actually said** ("slop").
- **Undisclosed AI.** It feels like a trick when people find out.
- **Tragic, political or divisive headlines** that "lead to arguments" (DNR 2025).
- **Dead air:** the TV idle for more than about 3 s, or phones idle for more than about 10 s.
- **One person dominating** the pitches or the votes.
- **Cruel roasts or humiliating Shame Titles.**
- **Needing everyone live for a long session** (Clubhouse).
- **One-trick novelty with no fresh content** (Gas, Among Us). The weekly headline is the antidote.
- **A rules tutorial up front.** Teach through the first scene instead.
