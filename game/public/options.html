Core mechanics menu

Every central piece of Group Project and every way it could work, in no particular order. No recommendations here; this is just the menu.

Character creation

How you become a hero.

  1. Tap a ready-made cartoon hero (instant) now
  2. Describe a hero in one line; the AI writes and draws it (~40 s) now
  3. Ready-made, plus 2 questions: "weirdly good at" becomes your power, "small fear" your weakness you
  4. Only the 2 questions; the AI builds the whole hero from you
  5. "One true thing" about you, used once as a nod replaced
  6. Partner's 3 questions (good at / scared of / could talk about for hours)
  7. Selfie-based hero rejected: uncomfortable
  8. Random hero, reroll once
  9. No heroes: you play yourself

Whose action happens

Each beat of the story, whose idea moves it forward?

  1. Everyone acts at once; every plan happens live site "too much going on"
  2. Group picks one plan by vote "my answer never chosen"
  3. Vote, but you can't back your own + spotlight rule (a winner can't win again until everyone has)
  4. Turn-based: on your turn you type and roll, others watch you
  5. Turn-based: everyone suggests for the turn owner, the owner picks Claude
  6. Turn-based: everyone suggests, the table votes, the owner rolls
  7. Rotating leader each scene, others add on
  8. Pairs: two players team up on one action

Dice vs no dice

How we find out if it worked.

  1. One d20 roll per action + modifiers (classic D&D) you
  2. Roll only when the outcome is uncertain; obvious things just happen (real DM rule)
  3. No dice: the AI decides from how good the idea is AI judging = bad feel
  4. "Clever = no roll", everything else rolls now
  5. Everyone rolls, best die counts "5 chances at a 20, no good"
  6. Final shared roll: everyone shakes, one die you
  7. Card draw / spinner instead of dice

Who judges if a plan is reasonable

Difficulty, before the roll.

  1. The AI sets difficulty tiers (solid / risky / wild / miracle) + a one-line reason now
  2. The AI's ruling + "Convince the DM" (argue once; can only go up) you liked it
  3. Fixed difficulty per scene; no ruling
  4. The table votes on difficulty
  5. The AI sets difficulty, and friends can lower it by backing the plan

Where stories come from

The seed of each game.

  1. Curated odd real news, hand-picked by us now (8 stories)
  2. Live news pulled tonight (SerpApi) and filtered
  3. Local news first: city, then state, then national you kept it
  4. Players bring their own story ("my flight got cancelled in Lisbon") Meta rep
  5. Players pick a setting / kind of chaos Meta rep
  6. Heavy real news with guardrails (future mode) pitch only

Picking tonight's story

Who decides which story you play.

  1. Host taps a story on the board now
  2. Fully random
  3. 3 random headlines, everyone votes you chose
  4. The AI picks the best fit for the group "unfair advantage"
  5. 3 random + a "write your own" card
  6. Roll for it: one d20 picks among 3

How the story text is written

Who writes the words on screen.

  1. The AI writes everything live "unfollowable"
  2. We hand-write the scenes; the AI writes only outcomes you chose
  3. The AI writes ahead of time; we review and edit
  4. The AI writes ahead from a player's own story, using a strong model, while people vote
  5. Fully hand-written, branching paths (no AI writing)
  6. Which model: Muse Spark (fast, funnier in testing) vs Muse Wonder (bigger, slower) vs a non-Meta model "test models, show taste"

Using each hero's quirks

How powers and weaknesses show up.

  1. Every outcome mentions every hero's quirk too much at 4+ players
  2. Only the hero whose turn it is you
  3. Only when the plan uses it
  4. Each hero gets one spotlight scene written for their power
  5. The weakness shows up only on a failed roll, as comedy
  6. The reveal names each real quality: "Solved by Ava's parallel parking"

Length and structure

How long a game lasts.

  1. Quick Tale: one scene, about 3 min now
  2. Full Story: 3 scenes (hook, twist, climax), 8+ min now
  3. Turn count = player count (1 turn each), the story bends to fit
  4. 2 turns each, spread across 3 scenes
  5. Fixed clock (e.g. 5 min) and the story wraps up when time's up
  6. Weekly chapters, continuing next week pitch only

Connection moments between players

Mechanics that make you turn to your friend.

  1. None: just talking out loud while playing
  2. Private clues: one only you noticed now
  3. Jigsaw clues: your clue helps a friend's power
  4. Suggest an action for a friend's hero
  5. Help: add one friend's idea for +2
  6. Inspiration: the AI gives a token for a great idea; you give it to a friend
  7. Thank-you button for whoever saved or helped you now
  8. Catch!: save a friend's failed roll "not in D&D"
  9. Boost a friend's plan popularity contest
  10. Mirror, Mirror: one kind line about a friend "weak link"
  11. Emoji reactions on the TV now
  12. Shake together for the final roll now

Stakes and winning

What you're playing for.

  1. Team vs the story: you win or lose together now
  2. Hope meter (goes up and down, shapes the ending) now, hidden in demo
  3. Ending tiers: You did it / Mostly fine / Well, it's a story now
  4. No fail state: it always ends funny
  5. Score per player no rating people

The ending

The last 60 seconds.

  1. "Official story vs what you uncovered" reveal now
  2. "This really happened. Here's the article." (proof, not twist)
  3. "Solved by Ava's parallel parking and Ben's coffee"
  4. Group poster with all your heroes built
  5. Keepsake link + QR to take home now
  6. Short storybook (1-2 pages) vs the long book (8 pages) long one too slow
  7. Shelf of past stories on the TV

Narration

How the story reaches your eyes and ears.

  1. Text only
  2. Text appears line by line at reading speed
  3. A voice reads it aloud (Gemini, ElevenLabs, OpenAI); samples coming you
  4. Voice for scenes only (pre-recorded) vs also live outcomes
  5. Music and sound effects under everything now

Art

What the TV shows.

  1. Pre-painted scene per story now
  2. Your heroes painted into the scenes, prepared in the background built
  3. Paint a new scene live after every action (slow, 15 s)
  4. Heroes as cards beside the scene only

How players input

What you do on your phone.

  1. Type (default) now
  2. Voice, with talking to the phone optional now
  3. Pick from AI suggestions "answers all the same"
  4. Pick from friends' suggestions
  5. Buttons only (fast, less creative)

Showing what the AI does

Making the AI explainable.

  1. DM's Notes panel: every decision and why now
  2. A one-line reason under each ruling now
  3. Judges get their own live link to the notes now
  4. Hidden: just play

Where you play

The setup.

  1. One room: laptop or TV + phones now
  2. Remote friends watch a /watch big screen built
  3. Share the TV screen on FaceTime
  4. A phone hosts, with no laptop (partner's branch)

Pacing

How the game keeps moving.

  1. Timers on every step now
  2. Move on as soon as everyone's done
  3. The host presses Continue
  4. Autopilot for players who walk away built
  5. Auto-roll if you don't roll, plus a buzz you

Who it's for

Audience and safety.

  1. Kids / Teens / Adults setting partner, kept
  2. One fixed tone for everyone
  3. Nothing in real life outside the game (no meetups) you
  4. Filters on news and on what players type now

The Meta judge's lens for all of it: "the flow should just work really well", "not about how many features", AI that feels "magical", not an afterthought.
