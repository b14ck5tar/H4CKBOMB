Group Project

A party game where tonight's weirdest real news becomes a story your friends fix together. Meta Muse runs the table.

Real news: an 80-ton boulder that hung over a New Hampshire park for 100 years finally fell. Nobody was hurt.
In the game: the boulder is still warm, and it's breathing.

How it plays

1 · JoinScan the code on the TV. Pick a cartoon hero. Optional: one thing you're weirdly good at and one small fear. They become your power and weakness.
2 · Pick a storyThree real, odd headlines. The table votes.
3 · Make a planEveryone types what their hero does. You back a friend's plan, not your own.
4 · RollThe AI rules how hard it is and says why. The winner rolls one die. The whole table watches.
5 · The reveal"Here's the official story. Here's what your group uncovered." You leave with a keepsake link.

Why it brings people closer

  • People talk to each other, not to the AI. The AI is the host. Phones are for typing; the fun is the room.
  • You have to look at your friends' ideas. You can't back your own plan, and a "spotlight rule" stops any one player from winning every round.
  • You learn something real about them. "Solved by Ava's parallel parking and Ben's coffee."
  • Nobody gets rated. The AI judges ideas, never people.

What the AI does, and what it doesn't

Muse does

  • Turns a real headline into a safe, funny story
  • Draws the cartoon heroes and scenes
  • Rules each plan: easy, risky or wild, with a plain reason
  • Narrates what happens, using your exact words

Code and people do

  • Dice: real randomness in code. The AI never picks numbers
  • Friends choose whose plan goes
  • Safety filters on news and on what players type
  • "DM's Notes" shows every AI decision, live

What we learned testing today (honest)

  • Too much at once. We built too many features. We're cutting to one simple round: plan, back a friend, roll, see what happens.
  • The AI's writing was hard to follow. We're hand-writing each story's spine in plain English. The AI only reacts to what players do.
  • Suggested answers made everyone sound the same. Removed. Players write their own.
  • Your hero wasn't in the art. Next: your cartoon heroes appear in every scene, plus a group poster at the end.

"Small steps beat big leaps." We heard that in your talk. That's tonight's plan.

Questions for you
